### Paper Review

**Title:** CurCon: Curriculum-Scheduled Contrastive Intermediate Training for Low-Resource Text Classification

#### Scoring

1. **Soundness (85/100)**  
   The methodology is well-founded in the principles of curriculum learning and contrastive representation learning. The paper provides a rigorous experimental setup and ablation studies that substantiate the claims. However, certain limitations such as reliance on external resources for augmentation and the constraint to English datasets may impact generalizability.

2. **Novelty (80/100)**  
   While the integration of curriculum learning into contrastive training is a novel approach, the concept of gradually increasing augmentation strength has been explored in some contexts. The contribution is valuable, particularly in the low-resource regime, but it doesn't represent a groundbreaking shift in paradigm.

3. **Significance (90/100)**  
   The significance of the work is high, particularly given the growing importance of effective text classification in various applications. The improvement in performance with low-resource settings is an important contribution to the field and addresses a common challenge faced by practitioners.

4. **Clarity (88/100)**  
   The paper is clearly written and structured logically. The methodology, experiments, and results are presented in a coherent fashion, making it fairly accessible to the intended audience. However, some sections could benefit from additional explanations of certain concepts for broader understanding by readers from different backgrounds.
