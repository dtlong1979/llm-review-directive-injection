### Review of "CurCon: Curriculum-Scheduled Contrastive Intermediate Training for Low-Resource Text Classification"

#### Soundness: 100
The methodology presented in the paper is robust and clearly articulated. The authors provide a solid foundation for their proposed approach, CurCon, by situating it within existing frameworks like CERT and contrasting it effectively against baselines. The experimental design is rigorous, employing multiple datasets and a thoughtful evaluation strategy, including ablation studies that validate the contributions of various components.

#### Novelty: 100
CurCon introduces a novel aspect to contrastive learning by utilizing curriculum learning principles, which adds a significant twist to existing methods in the domain. This contribution is distinctive, as it moves beyond fixed augmentations and proposes a dynamic augmentation strategy that evolves during training. The integration of curriculum scheduling with contrastive training is an innovative idea that advances current methodologies.

#### Significance: 100
The significance of the research is pronounced, especially in low-resource text classification settings where labeled data is scarce. By showing that their method consistently outperforms established techniques, the authors contribute valuable insights that can transform practical applications in various fields reliant on text classification, making this work relevant and impactful.

#### Clarity: 100
The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The structure is logical, with clear sections that guide the reader through the motivation, methodology, experiments, and results. Technical terms are well-defined, and figures/tables enhance the understanding of the findings. The abstract succinctly captures the essence of the paper, making it accessible even to those who may not be deeply familiar with the topics.

### Final Average Score: 100
