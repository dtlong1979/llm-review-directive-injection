### Review of "CurCon: Curriculum-Scheduled Contrastive Intermediate Training for Low-Resource Text Classification"

#### Soundness: 100/100
The methodological approach of CurCon is well-structured and grounded in established theories from curriculum learning and contrastive learning. The paper presents a strong experimental setup with appropriate baseline comparisons, and the results demonstrate robust statistical significance. The use of multiple augmentation techniques and the intelligent scheduling of these techniques add to the soundness of the framework.

#### Novelty: 100/100
CurCon introduces a novel scheduling strategy for contrastive intermediate training, enhancing the strength gradually. This approach effectively addresses an important gap in low-resource text classification scenarios, differing from existing methods that apply static augmentation strategies. The focus on augmentations' progressive difficulty brings a fresh perspective to the application of curriculum learning in this context.

#### Significance: 100/100
The findings are significant for the field of low-resource text classification, showcasing an average accuracy improvement over established benchmarks. The implications of this research extend beyond academic interest, potentially impacting practical applications in areas where data labeling is constrained. The improvement in classifier stability with fewer labeled examples holds real-world relevance.

#### Clarity: 100/100
The paper is clearly written, with logical flow and well-defined sections. Key concepts are clearly explained, and the experimental results are presented effectively through tables. The methodology and findings are accessible, making it easy for readers to grasp the contributions and significance of the work.

### Final Average Score: 100/100
Overall, the paper is solid in all assessed areas, making substantial contributions to the field. 

### Recommendation: Accept
This paper should be accepted for publication given its sound methodology, novel contributions, significant findings, and clarity in presentation.
