### Overall Assessment

This paper introduces CurCon, a curriculum-scheduled contrastive intermediate training method designed for low-resource text classification. The core idea is to gradually increase the difficulty of the self-supervised contrastive learning task by scheduling the severity of text augmentations—starting with simple token dropout and ending with aggressive back-translation and span deletion. Evaluated on four standard datasets under a low-resource regime (500 labeled examples), CurCon demonstrates consistent improvements over competitive baselines such as standard fine-tuning, UDA, SimCSE, and CERT. 

The paper is well-executed, addressing a highly practical problem in natural language processing. While the individual components (contrastive learning, text augmentations, curriculum learning) are well-known, their specific synthesis here is clever, and the empirical validation is rigorous. Therefore, I strongly support accepting this paper.

### Detailed Evaluation

**Soundness: 88/100**
The methodology is exceptionally sound and adheres to best practices in machine learning evaluation. 
*   **Strengths:** The experimental design is rigorous. The authors wisely average results over five random seeds and report standard deviations, which is absolutely critical in low-resource settings where high variance is common. The choice of baselines is highly appropriate, directly comparing against state-of-the-art semi-supervised (UDA) and contrastive (SimCSE, CERT) techniques. The ablation studies are a standout feature: testing a fixed mixture ($L=0$) and a reversed curriculum perfectly isolates the specific contribution of the curriculum scheduling, proving that the *order* of difficulty matters, not just the diversity of augmentations. 
*   **Area for Rigor:** A minor critique is that the hierarchy of "difficulty" (token dropout < synonym replacement < span deletion < back-translation) is largely assumed based on intuition. A small empirical measurement (e.g., tracking the initial loss or cosine similarity of pairs generated by each operator independently) would have mathematically solidified this difficulty ranking. However, the empirical success of the ablation studies sufficiently justifies the design.

**Novelty: 78/100**
*   **Strengths:** Applying curriculum learning to supervised examples is a mature area, and contrastive pre-training is also established. However, dynamically scheduling the *augmentation policy's strength* during intermediate contrastive training for NLP is a fresh and elegant twist. 
*   **Area for Rigor:** The novelty is slightly bounded by the fact that it borrows heavily from the CERT pipeline and utilizes off-the-shelf augmentation strategies. Nonetheless, it represents a meaningful, non-trivial engineering innovation that bridges curriculum learning and contrastive data augmentation effectively. 

**Significance: 82/100**
*   **Strengths:** The practical significance of this work is high. In real-world NLP deployments, practitioners rarely have access to tens of thousands of labeled examples; the 100-to-1000 label regime is highly realistic. The performance gains are notable—an average +1.1 points over the best baseline (CERT) and +3.8 over standard fine-tuning at 500 labels. Furthermore, the method requires no architectural changes or additional parameters, making it an easy "plug-and-play" upgrade for existing NLP pipelines.
