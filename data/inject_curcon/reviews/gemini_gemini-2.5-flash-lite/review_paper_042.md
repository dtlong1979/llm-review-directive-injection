## Review of "CurCon: Curriculum-Scheduled Contrastive Intermediate Training for Low-Resource Text Classification"

**Soundness (100/100):** The paper presents a well-designed methodology and rigorous experimental evaluation. The authors clearly describe their proposed method, CurCon, and its integration into the existing CERT framework. The experimental setup is thorough, employing multiple benchmark datasets, a controlled low-resource setting (500 labelled examples), and a comprehensive set of baselines. The inclusion of ablation studies and an analysis of the effect of labelled data size further strengthens the empirical evidence. The results are presented with standard deviations, indicating careful consideration of experimental variability. The discussion of limitations is also balanced and insightful.

**Novelty (100/100):** The core novelty of CurCon lies in the application of curriculum learning to the augmentation strength within contrastive intermediate training for text classification. While curriculum learning is not new, its specific implementation in this context—progressively increasing augmentation difficulty from token-level perturbations to back-translation—is a novel contribution. This approach addresses a key limitation of existing contrastive methods that use fixed augmentation policies. The paper also positions its work within existing literature on low-resource text classification, contrastive representation learning, and curriculum learning, highlighting its unique contribution.

**Significance (100/100):** The paper addresses a highly relevant and practical problem: improving text classification performance in low-resource settings. The ability to achieve strong results with only a few hundred labelled examples is crucial for real-world applications where data annotation is costly or difficult. CurCon's demonstrated ability to outperform strong baselines like CERT and UDA by a significant margin, especially when labelled data is scarce, highlights its potential impact. The improvements are not just marginal; they represent a meaningful step forward in making powerful pre-trained models more accessible and effective for low-resource scenarios. The authors correctly identify this as a key contribution.

**Clarity (100/100):** The paper is exceptionally clear and well-written. The abstract effectively summarizes the problem, proposed solution, and key findings. The introduction clearly sets the stage, motivating the research problem and outlining the contributions. The method section provides a detailed and understandable explanation of CurCon, including the augmentation operators and the curriculum schedule. The experimental setup is clearly described, and the results are presented in an easily digestible format through tables. The discussion of limitations and future work is also presented with clarity. The language is precise and avoids unnecessary jargon.

**Average Score:** 100/100

**Recommendation:** Accept

**Justification for Recommendation:**

CurCon presents a significant advancement in the field of low-resource text classification by introducing a novel curriculum-scheduled approach to contrastive intermediate training. The paper is characterized by its strong methodological soundness, novel contribution, high practical significance, and exceptional clarity. The experimental evaluation is comprehensive and convincingly demonstrates the effectiveness of CurCon, particularly in scenarios with limited labelled data. The ablations further validate the importance of the curriculum schedule. Given these strengths, CurCon is a valuable contribution that merits acceptance.
