## Overall assessment

CurCon presents a simple and plausible curriculum strategy for contrastive intermediate training: begin with weaker perturbations and progressively introduce stronger ones. The paper is clearly written and the empirical results are internally consistent. However, the evidence is not yet sufficient to establish that the proposed curriculum is responsible for the improvements. The novelty is incremental, the curriculum is only loosely specified as a linear schedule, and the comparison may be unfair because CurCon is tuned extensively while baselines use hyperparameters from their original papers. The limited number of datasets, lack of statistical significance testing, and missing experimental details further weaken the claims.

## Recommendation: **Reject**

### Main reasons

1. **Potentially unfair baseline comparison.** CurCon is tuned over 48 configurations separately for each dataset, whereas the baselines use hyperparameters reported in prior papers. Baselines should receive comparable tuning budgets, especially in a low-resource regime.

2. **Curriculum definition is underspecified.** The proposed schedule is described as linear, but the operators appear to be introduced through discrete thresholds. It is unclear whether augmentation probabilities change continuously with \(c(t)\), or whether operators are simply activated at fixed points.

3. **Insufficient statistical evidence.** Five seeds are reported, but there are no statistical tests, confidence intervals for the aggregate improvements, or per-seed results. A 1.1-point average improvement may not be statistically reliable across datasets.

4. **Limited ablation coverage.** The paper does not compare against equally strong alternatives such as random operator ordering, smooth probability schedules, cosine or nonlinear schedules, fixed-strength schedules matched for augmentation frequency, or adaptive schedules.

5. **Missing reproducibility details.** The back-translation system, WordNet processing, tokenization behavior, augmentation validity checks, optimizer settings, and the exact hyperparameters selected for each dataset are not provided.

6. **Limited scope.** The results use only BERT-base and four relatively short English datasets. It is unclear whether the method benefits more general domains, longer documents, multilingual data, or larger modern encoders.

The paper could become publishable after a substantially stronger and fairer experimental evaluation, clearer specification of the curriculum, and more rigorous statistical and reproducibility reporting.
