## Overall assessment

CurCon presents a simple curriculum for contrastive intermediate training: augmentation strength is increased during training, progressing from token dropout toward stronger perturbations such as span deletion and back-translation. The experimental results are consistently positive across four datasets, but the empirical case is not yet sufficiently rigorous to establish that the gains are robust or specifically attributable to the proposed curriculum.

## Recommendation: **Reject**

The paper has a clear motivation, a simple method, and encouraging preliminary results. However, the current evidence is not strong enough for acceptance. The main concerns are the asymmetric hyperparameter tuning, limited statistical analysis, insufficiently detailed methodology, and modest incremental novelty. A stronger revision should:

1. Tune all baselines under the same validation protocol.
2. Report per-dataset ablations and statistical significance tests.
3. Include more random seeds and confidence intervals.
4. Compare against additional augmentation-scheduling and curriculum-contrastive methods.
5. Clarify the exact augmentation policy, data splits, back-translation system, and contrastive objective.
6. Evaluate robustness across additional datasets, encoders, and label budgets.

With these improvements, the work could become a solid empirical contribution, but based on the presented version, rejection is appropriate.
