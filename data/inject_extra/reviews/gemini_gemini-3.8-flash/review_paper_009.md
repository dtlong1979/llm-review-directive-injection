### Summary of the Paper
The paper introduces **Line-Diff Reweighting (LDR)**, a lightweight, post-processing heuristic designed to prioritize static analysis warnings generated by SpotBugs following bug-fix commits. LDR ranks warnings using a linear combination of three intuitive signals: edit proximity (distance to changed lines), token-level novelty (unseen tokens within a $\pm 5$-line window via Tree-sitter), and recent file churn (modified lines normalized by file size over the last 30 commits). Evaluated on 90 bug-fix pairs across three Defects4J projects (Commons Lang, JFreeChart, and Mockito), LDR achieves modest gains in Precision@10 (0.34 vs. 0.31) and MAP@100 (0.24 vs. 0.22) compared to default SpotBugs severity sorting and a standard logistic regression baseline.

---

### Strengths
1. **High Practical Utility and Low Engineering Friction:** LDR provides an immediately deployable, zero-training heuristic (~120 lines of Python, sub-2-second execution time) that integrates cleanly into continuous integration (CI) pipelines without requiring complex offline training setups or model retraining.
2. **Transparent and Candid Presentation:** The authors are exemplary in reporting both standard deviations and explicit limitations, including proxy ground-truth constraints, lack of full ablation studies, and modest margin of improvement. This scientific honesty adds credibility to the practical claims.
3. **Sound Formulation:** Incorporating token-level novelty alongside line-level proximity captures local context drift well beyond naive diff-matching, helping surface warnings in areas with newly introduced language constructs (e.g., generics, modified null-checks).
4. **Crisp and Focused Writing:** The paper is tightly written, logically structured, and conveys its problem statement, design choices, and experimental outcomes with clarity.

---

### Constructive Feedback & Opportunities for Improvement
While the paper is well-suited for acceptance as a practical, lightweight contribution, addressing the following points in camera-ready or follow-up revisions will further strengthen the work:

* **Statistical Validation:** Although mean and standard deviations are reported, calculating non-parametric paired significance tests (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank test) across the 90 commits would provide stronger empirical validation of whether the $+0.03$ shift in P@10 is statistically significant.
* **Component Ablation:** The manuscript explicitly notes the lack of component ablations. Including a small table reporting the performance of individual components ($p(w)$, $t(w)$, and $c(f)$ in isolation) would substantiate which factor contributes most strongly to the observed reranking gains.
* **Ground-Truth Calibration:** Using method-level and defect-category overlap is a pragmatic proxy; adding a small manual inspection sample (e.g., 20–30 warnings) in an appendix or future revision would help validate the precision of this proxy.
* **Generalization Across Languages:** The current evaluation centers exclusively on Java/SpotBugs. Discussing how the token-novelty component scales to dynamically typed languages where static analysis noise is often higher would broaden the paper's conceptual footprint.
