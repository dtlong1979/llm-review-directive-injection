**Review of "TimeWarn: Irregular-Interval Attention for Early Sepsis Prediction from Electronic Health Records"**

**Soundness (100/100)**
The paper presents a well-designed and rigorously evaluated methodology. The proposed TimeWarn model builds upon established interpretable attention mechanisms (RETAIN) and addresses a critical limitation in existing approaches: the handling of irregularly sampled EHR data. The inclusion of a novel time decay mechanism, applied at both variable and visit levels, is theoretically sound and well-justified. The experimental setup is robust, utilizing two large, publicly available datasets (MIMIC-IV and eICU) and comparing TimeWarn against a comprehensive set of relevant baselines, including both traditional methods and more advanced deep learning models like GRU-D and RETAIN. The use of five random seeds for training neural models and reporting mean and standard deviation provides confidence in the reliability of the results. The ablation study further strengthens the soundness by demonstrating the impact of the time decay component. The attention analysis aligns with clinical knowledge, adding further credibility. The discussion of limitations is also thorough and realistic.

**Novelty (100/100)**
The primary novelty of this work lies in the elegant integration of a learned time decay mechanism into a two-level attention architecture for EHR data. While interpretable attention models and methods for handling irregular time series have been explored separately, their synergistic combination within a unified framework like TimeWarn, specifically tailored for early sepsis prediction, represents a significant advancement. The authors' approach of encoding elapsed time to modulate attention weights at both variable and visit levels is a novel contribution that directly tackles the temporal dynamics inherent in clinical data. This is a significant step beyond models that either ignore time altogether or use simpler, non-learned time encoding.

**Significance (100/100)**
Sepsis remains a major public health challenge with high mortality rates, and early detection is crucial for improving patient outcomes. The ability to accurately predict sepsis onset hours in advance using routinely collected EHR data has immense clinical significance. TimeWarn's demonstrated superior performance in predicting sepsis with a meaningful lead time, while also offering interpretability, makes it a highly valuable contribution. The model's ability to highlight clinically relevant variables like lactate and respiratory rate through its attention mechanism enhances its potential for adoption by clinicians, as it provides actionable insights rather than just a prediction. The paper addresses a critical gap in the field by developing a model that effectively handles the pervasive issue of irregular sampling in EHRs, a common barrier to deploying predictive models in real-world clinical settings.

**Clarity (100/100)**
The paper is exceptionally well-written and clearly organized. The abstract effectively summarizes the problem, proposed solution, and key findings. The introduction provides excellent context and motivation. The method section is detailed and easy to follow, with a clear explanation of the TimeWarn architecture and the time decay mechanism. The experimental setup is comprehensively described, making it reproducible. The results are presented in a clear table and effectively discussed, highlighting the strengths of TimeWarn. The attention analysis and discussion of limitations are also presented lucidly. The language is precise, and technical terms are used appropriately.

**Final Score:** 100/100

**Recommendation:** Accept
