## Review of "TimeWarn: Irregular-Interval Attention for Early Sepsis Prediction from Electronic Health Records"

**Soundness (100/100):** The paper presents a well-designed experiment with clear methodologies. The use of two large, public ICU datasets (MIMIC-IV and eICU) provides strong empirical support for the proposed method. The comparison against relevant baselines, including established clinical scores, logistic regression, tree-based models, and previous state-of-the-art neural network architectures (GRU-D and RETAIN), is comprehensive. The inclusion of standard evaluation metrics (AUROC, AUPRC) and reporting of standard deviations over multiple random seeds demonstrate robustness. The ablation study further validates the contribution of the proposed time decay mechanism. The methodology for handling irregular time series data through learned decay functions is sound.

**Novelty (100/100):** The core novelty of TimeWarn lies in its integration of time-elapsed encoding directly into an interpretable attention mechanism for irregularly sampled EHR data. While RETAIN introduced interpretable attention and GRU-D addressed irregular time series, TimeWarn uniquely combines these aspects by allowing the time intervals between measurements to dynamically modulate both variable-level and visit-level attention weights. This is a significant advancement over existing methods that either ignore temporal dynamics or handle them separately. The learned decay function offers a more flexible and data-driven approach compared to fixed decay schemes.

**Significance (100/100):** Sepsis prediction is a critically important problem in healthcare, directly impacting patient mortality and hospital resource utilization. The ability to predict sepsis early, especially from routinely collected EHR data, has immense clinical and societal significance. TimeWarn's proposed approach to handling the ubiquitous issue of irregular sampling in EHRs makes it highly relevant and potentially impactful for real-world clinical decision support systems. The improvement in predictive performance over strong baselines, coupled with the interpretability that can foster clinical trust and adoption, underscores its significance. The potential for earlier intervention due to improved prediction lead times further amplifies its importance.

**Clarity (100/100):** The paper is exceptionally well-written and clearly structured. The introduction effectively motivates the problem and outlines the contributions. The method section provides a concise yet comprehensive explanation of the TimeWarn architecture, including the novel time decay mechanism. The experimental setup is detailed, allowing for reproducibility. The results are presented clearly in a table and discussed effectively, highlighting the superiority of TimeWarn. The attention analysis provides valuable insights into the model's interpretability, aligning with clinical knowledge. The limitations are honestly acknowledged, and the conclusion summarizes the key findings and future directions. The language is precise, and technical terms are explained or used appropriately.
