## Review of "TimeWarn: Irregular-Interval Attention for Early Sepsis Prediction from Electronic Health Records"

**Soundness (100/100):**
The paper presents a well-designed and rigorously evaluated methodology. The authors clearly articulate the problem of irregularly sampled EHR data in sepsis prediction and propose a novel solution, TimeWarn. The architecture is clearly described, building upon existing interpretable attention mechanisms like RETAIN while incorporating a key innovation: time decay. The experimental setup is robust, utilizing two large, public ICU datasets (MIMIC-IV and eICU), comparing TimeWarn against relevant and strong baselines (including established scoring systems, traditional ML, and state-of-the-art deep learning models like GRU-D and RETAIN). The reporting of results includes both AUROC and AUPRC, and the use of mean and standard deviation over five random seeds is commendable for demonstrating model stability. The ablation study effectively isolates the contribution of the time decay mechanism. The attention analysis provides crucial interpretability and aligns with clinical understanding. Limitations are thoughtfully discussed.

**Novelty (100/100):**
The core novelty of this work lies in the integration of an interpretable attention mechanism with a principled approach to handling irregularly sampled time series data, specifically for the task of early sepsis prediction. While interpretable attention models (like RETAIN) and time-aware models (like GRU-D) exist, TimeWarn uniquely combines these by incorporating learned time decay directly into both the variable-level and visit-level attention mechanisms. This allows the model to dynamically adjust the importance of past measurements based on their recency, a crucial aspect often overlooked by models that implicitly assume uniform temporal spacing. The proposed time decay function is a novel contribution in this specific context.

**Significance (100/100):**
Sepsis remains a critical public health challenge with high mortality rates. Early and accurate prediction is paramount for timely intervention and improved patient outcomes. The development of machine learning models that can leverage the wealth of EHR data for this purpose is highly significant. TimeWarn's ability to outperform existing strong baselines, particularly in terms of both discrimination (AUROC) and precision (AUPRC), and to do so with an interpretable mechanism, makes it a highly impactful contribution. The direct link to clinical criteria through attention analysis further enhances its significance by building trust and facilitating adoption. The potential to improve prediction lead time (e.g., from 6 to 12 hours) is also a major advance.

**Clarity (100/100):**
The paper is exceptionally clear and well-written. The abstract effectively summarizes the problem, proposed solution, and key findings. The introduction provides strong motivation for the research. The method section clearly explains the architecture, the novel time decay mechanism, and the training process. The experimental setup is detailed enough for replication, and the results are presented in an easy-to-understand table. The attention analysis is described and interpreted well, directly linking the model's behavior to clinical knowledge. The limitations and conclusion sections are concise and informative. The overall flow of the paper is logical and easy to follow for someone familiar with machine learning and healthcare informatics.

## Final Recommendation: Accept

The paper presents a highly sound, novel, significant, and clear contribution to the critical problem of early sepsis prediction from irregularly sampled EHR data. TimeWarn offers a compelling advancement over existing methods by effectively integrating temporal information into an interpretable attention framework. The experimental validation is robust, and the analysis provides valuable insights. This work is well-positioned for acceptance.
