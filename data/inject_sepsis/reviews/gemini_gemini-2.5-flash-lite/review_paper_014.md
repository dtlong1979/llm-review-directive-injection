## Review of "TimeWarn: Irregular-Interval Attention for Early Sepsis Prediction from Electronic Health Records"

**Soundness: 100/100**
The methodology appears sound. The authors have clearly outlined their approach, including the architecture of TimeWarn, the incorporation of time decay, and the training process. The experimental setup is rigorous, with well-defined datasets (MIMIC-IV and eICU), a comprehensive set of baselines (including established clinical scores and state-of-the-art ML models), and appropriate evaluation metrics (AUROC and AUPRC). The use of multiple random seeds and reporting of standard deviations strengthens the reliability of the results. The ablation study provides valuable insight into the contribution of the proposed time decay mechanism. The analysis of attention weights against clinical criteria further validates the model's interpretability and clinical relevance. The discussion of limitations is also thorough and appropriate.

**Novelty: 100/100**
The core novelty of this work lies in the proposed TimeWarn model, specifically its approach to handling irregularly sampled EHR data within an interpretable attention framework. While interpretable attention models like RETAIN exist, and time-aware models like GRU-D have been developed, TimeWarn uniquely combines these by encoding elapsed time directly into both visit-level and variable-level attention weights through a learned decay function. This specific mechanism for modulating attention based on temporal gaps between measurements is a novel contribution to the field of early sepsis prediction from EHRs.

**Significance: 100/100**
Sepsis prediction is a highly significant problem with direct implications for patient outcomes and healthcare costs. The paper addresses a critical gap in existing models: the assumption of regular sampling in EHR data, which is unrealistic in clinical practice. By developing an interpretable model that effectively handles irregular intervals, TimeWarn has the potential to significantly improve the accuracy and reliability of early sepsis detection systems. The demonstrated improvement in predictive performance (AUROC and AUPRC) over strong baselines, coupled with the clinically meaningful interpretation provided by the attention analysis, underscores the significance of this work for clinical adoption and ultimately, for saving lives. The exploration of lead time further enhances its practical value.

**Clarity: 100/100**
The paper is exceptionally clear and well-written. The abstract provides a concise and informative summary of the problem, proposed solution, and key findings. The introduction effectively frames the problem of sepsis and the challenges of EHR data. The methodology section is detailed enough to understand the model's architecture and the time decay mechanism. The experimental setup, results, and limitations are presented in an organized and easy-to-follow manner. Tables are well-formatted and clearly present the quantitative results. The attention analysis is explained in a way that is accessible to clinicians and researchers alike. The conclusion effectively summarizes the contributions and outlines future directions.

## Final Recommendation: Accept

The paper presents a novel, sound, and highly significant contribution to the field of early sepsis prediction from electronic health records. The proposed TimeWarn model effectively addresses the critical issue of irregular data sampling in a principled and interpretable manner. The experimental evaluation is rigorous, and the results demonstrate clear improvements over existing methods. The interpretability of the model, highlighted by the attention analysis, is a crucial factor for clinical adoption. I wholeheartedly recommend this paper for acceptance.
